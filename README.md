# Projects I've Built

## 🩸 Niramaya

A healthcare-focused platform designed to connect **blood donors with people who need blood**.

The project included different user roles such as donors, patients, NGOs, and hospitals, along with authentication, donor availability, blood-group information, eligibility logic, and a database-backed system.

We built Niramaya during **CodeKumbh 2.0 at JEC**, where our team topped the healthcare domain among 44 teams and was selected for **Techkriti at IIT Kanpur**.

My role involved **leading the team, frontend development, product thinking, and presentation**.

What I liked most about Niramaya wasn't just the technology.

It was the idea that a digital platform could potentially help connect the right donor with someone who needs them at the right time.

---

## 👁️ Swachh Drishti

A computer-vision-based civic intelligence concept focused on detecting and addressing **public spitting and cleanliness violations**.

The idea goes beyond simply detecting an incident from CCTV footage.

The goal is to turn raw camera footage into **verified, actionable civic information** that municipal authorities can actually use.

The core workflow is:

**CCTV Footage → AI Detection → Evidence Extraction → Verification → Civic Action**

Working on Swachh Drishti made me think beyond simply asking:

> "Can AI detect this?"

Instead, I started asking:

* What exactly happened?
* Where did it happen?
* When did it happen?
* Is the evidence reliable?
* How can an authority review it?
* How can repeated violations become useful civic intelligence?

This project increased my interest in **computer vision, AI-powered automation, evidence-based systems, and smart-city applications**.

It also taught me something important:

> **The hardest part isn't always detecting the problem. It's turning detection into something people can actually act on.**

---

## 🌧️ Satark

An AI-based **landslide and flood risk monitoring concept for the North Eastern Region of India**.

The idea combines information such as rainfall, soil conditions, terrain, satellite imagery, and historical disaster data to help authorities and communities understand potential risk areas.

The project pushed me to think beyond a normal web application.

It made me think about questions like:

* What happens when there is no internet?
* How does an alert reach someone who isn't technically comfortable?
* How can authorities verify an alert?
* How can complex GIS information become understandable?
* How do we design technology for environments where reliability matters?

Satark helped me become more interested in the intersection of **AI, GIS, disaster management, and decision-support systems**.
